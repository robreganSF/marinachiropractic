About chiropractic

A short introduction to how chiropractors think about the spine, and what care typically involves.

Why people see a chiropractor

Chiropractic care is most commonly sought for musculoskeletal complaints — back pain, neck pain, headaches, joint stiffness, and the everyday strain that comes from desks, commutes, training, and sleep. Patients range from infants to the elderly.

Evidence for chiropractic care is strongest for low back pain, neck pain, and certain headaches. Whether it will help your particular situation is exactly the sort of thing worth discussing on a call before you book — Dr. Neely will tell you honestly if you would be better served by someone else.

Spinal alignment and joint motion

Proper spinal alignment supports comfortable, pain-free movement. When a joint is injured, one of the ways the body responds is by stabilising the area — over time, calcium can build up around it, and the joint may stiffen. This splinting is the body's attempt to protect an injured joint, but the loss of normal motion can create its own problems.

Chiropractic care aims to restore normal motion to joints that have become restricted, with the goal of reducing pain and improving how you move.

What chiropractors mean by "subluxation"

You will hear the term subluxation used often in chiropractic. In this context it refers to a vertebra that has lost its normal position or normal range of motion relative to its neighbours, which chiropractors associate with irritation of nearby soft tissue and nerves.

Chiropractors generally describe several components:

  • Spinal kinesiopathology — joints of the spine that move too little or too much.
  • Neuropathophysiology — irritation of nerve tissue associated with abnormal spinal function.
  • Myopathology — muscles supporting the spine becoming imbalanced, weakened, or overdeveloped on one side.
  • Histopathology — inflammation and swelling in the surrounding soft tissue.
  • Pathophysiology — longer-term changes such as calcium deposition around a joint that has been injured.

It is worth being straightforward about this: the subluxation model is a framework used within the chiropractic profession, and parts of it remain debated in the wider medical literature. Dr. Neely is happy to talk through what she finds in your case and why she is recommending what she recommends.

Practical tips for a healthier back

Most of what protects your spine happens outside a treatment room:

  • Move regularly. Nothing strenuous is required — a daily walk makes a real difference.
  • Eat well. Good nutrition gives the body what it needs to repair itself.
  • Mind your posture. Are you sitting up straight reading this?
  • Stretch before and after sport, to loosen the muscles surrounding the spine.
  • Do not overload a bag or backpack, and carry it across both shoulders where you can.
  • Get up every hour of sitting, whether at a desk or in a car, and stretch your legs and back.
  • Never cradle a phone between your neck and shoulder.
  • Sleep on your back or side rather than your stomach, which keeps the spine better aligned overnight.
  • Invest in a good chair, pillow, and mattress. Given the hours you spend using them, it is money well spent.
  • Have regular check-ups. Preventing a problem is easier than correcting one.
